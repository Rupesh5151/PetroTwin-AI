# PETRO-TWIN AI: Petroleum Engineering & Heavy Oil Domain Foundations
## Baghewala Field Heavy Oil Exploitation in Jodhpur Sandstone

---

### 1. Reservoir Geology & Petrophysics
The **Baghewala Field**, situated in the Bikaner-Nagaur Basin of Western Rajasthan, represents India's primary onshore continental heavy oil accumulation.
* **Stratigraphic Unit:** Jodhpur Sandstone (Late Proterozoic / Early Cambrian sequence).
* **Depth:** Shallow, between 900 meters to 1,100 meters True Vertical Depth (TVD).
* **Lithology:** Highly friable, fine-to-medium grained quartzose sandstone with interbedded shales and silts.
* **Porosity ($\phi$):** High, averaging 24% to 28% (mean ~26%).
* **Permeability ($k$):** Moderate to high, ranging from 450 mD to 1,800 mD (mean ~850 mD).
* **Initial Pore Pressure ($P_{\text{initial}}$):** Abnormally depleted (~45 to 65 bar, 0.48–0.65 bar/10m gradient).
* **Geothermal Temperature ($T_{\text{res}}$):** 46°C to 48°C.

---

### 2. PVT & Fluid Characterization
The crude oil discovered at Baghewala possesses extreme chemical and rheological complexity:
* **API Gravity:** 17.0° – 19.0° API (specific gravity ~0.940 – 0.952 g/cm³).
* **Native Dead Oil Viscosity:**
  * At 46°C (Reservoir Conditions): **1,800 – 6,500+ cP** (in isolated intervals exceeding 10,000 cP).
  * At 100°C: **120 – 180 cP**.
  * At 200°C: **12 – 18 cP**.
* **Asphaltene Content:** Unusually elevated (16.0 – 22.5 wt%).
* **Wax Content:** 8.0 – 12.0 wt%.
* **Sulfur Content:** High (> 2.5 – 3.8 wt%).

Because of the massive asphaltene fraction, temperature drops below 70°C induce severe precipitation, flocculation, and structural gelation, causing drastic flow throttling in the near-wellbore matrix.

---

### 3. Thermodynamic Mechanism of CSS
Under Darcy's law for radial flow into a wellbore:
$$q_o = \frac{2 \pi k k_{ro} h (P_{\text{res}} - P_{wf})}{\mu_o \left[ \ln(r_e / r_w) + S \right]}$$

At 46°C, with $\mu_o \approx 4,200 \text{ cP}$ and depleted $P_{\text{res}} \approx 55 \text{ bar}$, primary natural inflow $q_o$ is less than 0.5 to 2.0 bbl/day, rendering unheated primary extraction economically infeasible.

**Cyclic Steam Stimulation operates by thermal viscosity reduction:**
1. **Sensible & Latent Heat Deposition:** Steam at 280°C–310°C is injected at 70–80 bar. The phase change from water vapor to boiling liquid releases the massive latent heat of vaporization ($L_v \approx 1,420 \text{ kJ/kg}$).
2. **Radial Heat Front:** Heat conducts and convects radially 15–35 meters into the Jodhpur Sandstone.
3. **Viscosity Collapse:** Viscosity plummets from 4,200 cP down to ~15 cP.
4. **Oil Mobility Surge:** With $\mu_o$ reduced by a factor of 280, mobility $\frac{k k_{ro}}{\mu_o}$ surges by over 250-fold, allowing flush production of 80 to 220 bbl/day during the early cycle.

### 4. Artificial Lift (SRP) Challenges in Cold Desert Wells
* Heavy oil cannot naturally ascend the casing string; an API Sucker Rod Pump is seated at ~900m depth.
* Over the 90–150 day production window, the reservoir continuously conducts heat away to non-productive shale caprock, dropping bottomhole temperature back towards 50°C.
* As viscosity rebounds, the fluid column in the 2-7/8" tubing becomes viscous sludge.
* **Mechanical Failure Cascades:**
  1. Excessive annular Couette shear drag.
  2. Plunger valve throttling.
  3. Rod string compression and buckling on the downstroke (**Rod Floating**).
  4. Slack bridle cables and massive tensile impact waves on the upstroke turnaround (**Impact Loading**).
  5. Fatigue failure at rod threads (**Parted Rods**).
  6. Bottomhole pump hydraulic upward unseating (**Pump Unsetting**).
